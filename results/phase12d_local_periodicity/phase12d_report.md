## 5. Recommendation for Phase 13's forward model

The step-size curve (Task A) shows the design-relevant reflection-phase error stays under the design tolerance for every tested step out to double digits of degrees away from resonance (measured up to 5.8 deg/cell), but every tested near- or at-resonance step -- the smallest achievable at this project's validated floquet convention, itself tens of degrees because a mesh fine enough to embed genuinely small (10-45 deg) steps THROUGH the resonance transition is computationally out of reach for a supercell solve -- already fails badly (19-173 deg phase error). So the true near-resonance crossover step remains bounded, not pinned to a single number, but the practical conclusion is the same either way: an aperture whose phase profile must pass through this design's resonance region cannot rely on a lookup table there. The aperture-level test (Task B), which actually decides Phase 13, confirms this independently from a full-supercell MoM solve (not the dipole proxy): at every tested size (N=6, 8, 12 cells per assumed cycle), the lookup-table-constructed anomalous reflector puts less than 53% of its reflected power in the intended order (measured: 52.9%, 41.1%, 47.5%) -- LOOKUP_TABLE_INADEQUATE, not merely 'adequate above some N'. The root cause is visible in the numbers: the lookup table's own achievable phase span at this design point (186 deg, Sec. 3) is far short of the full 360 deg a blazed grating needs, the same phase-coverage shortfall Phases 12A-12C already measured from a different angle. A full-aperture integral-equation solver (a la Budhu & Grbic 2021) is therefore the more broadly defensible default for this grounded 8 mm design point, not a lookup table constrained to small steps -- though a design confined entirely to the flat, off-resonance part of the curve (steps below ~6 deg/cell, Task A) remains a narrow case where a lookup table could still work. This is a recommendation, not a decision -- Phase 13 makes the actual choice, informed by both this phase's measured numbers and Phase 12C's own.
