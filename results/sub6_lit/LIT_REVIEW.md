## How this was done (read first)
* **No subscription access was available in this session.** I had web search and open-web fetch only; there was no IEEE Xplore, Wiley or Elsevier login. Paywalled papers are therefore known only from search-result abstracts or snippets. Every T1 row carries an `evidence` tag: **FULLTEXT** = I extracted and read the text (open PDF); **ABSTRACT** = search abstract/snippet only, numbers not checked against the paper; **BLOCKED** = publisher refused automated access (MDPI 403, JPIER bot check), snippet only.
* A snippet-level number is quoted only as "as reported by the snippet". Anything not measured is marked **SIMULATED_IN_PAPER**. Fields I could not find say `NOT_FOUND`; nothing was filled from memory.
* The search was shallow (about 25 queries). It is a starting list, not an exhaustive survey. **Finding: I found no paper that combines (surface-wave-fed holographic or leaky-wave) + (one PIN diode per cell) + (CP) + (two beam states) with measured data.** The nearest matches are listed below.

## T2. Cell concepts to simulate (ranked; one diode per cell, planar pattern + lumped load + optional vias)
**These are my proposals built from the papers above, not results from them. No source demonstrates a one-diode CP surface-wave cell, so all three are UNTESTED.**

| rank | concept | why it might beat the slotted disc + 2-gap cell | risks / what to check in MoM | sources |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Cross-slot (or notch) disc with the diode in one slot arm: 90-degree slot-axis flip per state.** The diode shorts or opens an arm so the effective axis rotates by 90 deg; the cell radiates in BOTH states with the same amplitude and opposite sign. | Efficiency: no cell is idle in either state (our current scheme silences the switched cells in OFF, giving eta_off 0.11-0.34). Our own round 2 found that 90-degree rotations are the lever (ON AR 3.49 -> 1.75 dB). Maps to a pure 1-bit phase. | The OFF = A+B, ON = A mapping needs a group/sign design, not the amplitude cancellation used now; the two arms need DC paths; asymmetric ON-state leakage. Check: Y tensor of both states, A_perp, whether alpha stays equal. | #7 (rotation sets polarisation), #14 (PIN gives 180 deg step), #1, #2 (binary phase) |
| 2 | **Single-gap ring or disc: one diode switches the scalar surface reactance between two values that both leak (low modulation index).** | Both states radiate (no silent state); only one diode (the 4-diode ring of #1 reduced to one gap); the ring is already a holographic-surface cell at 6.2 GHz. | Binary control lowers efficiency (modulation index M = 0.32 in #1); scalar cell does not control polarisation, so CP needs a second mechanism (rotated slot or tensor pattern). | #1 (164j / 84j ohm, BAR50 model), #3 |
| 3 | **Complementary ELC / slot cell with the diode across the neck, gated radiating vs silent** (the SIW version of #3 transplanted to a grounded slab). | Known to switch leakage strongly, so high ON/OFF contrast. | One state is silent, so this keeps our efficiency problem; the same two-half DC-path problem as now. Listed as a fallback, not an improvement. | #3, #2 |
Comparator: our slotted disc + notch (alpha ratio ON/OFF high but switched cells silent in OFF).

## T3. Bias options for a two-half cell (each half needs its own DC path)
**Measured bias penalties on the surface wave: NOT_FOUND in the literature I could reach.** The only quantitative statements are indirect.

| rank | option | measured penalty found | note / source |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Half A DC-grounded by a shorting via to the ground plane (the ground is the return); half B reached by a via to a bias layer under the ground through a clearance hole. Needs one signal line per cell, bias layer beneath the ground. | none isolated. #2 uses a bias layer next to the ground with zigzag/fan-capacitor chokes and measures 20.6 dBi at 68 % aperture efficiency with 128 diodes, i.e. the total effect of its bias design, not separable. | #2 (FULLTEXT). Vias are HFSS-only in our model: model the via as a lumped short plus clearance when estimating. |
| 2 | Shared/global bias: all halves of one type tied together (row or global bus), the other halves on a second bus; all cells in parallel gives one state for the whole surface | none (#4 is SIMULATED, gives no number) | #4 (FULLTEXT). Global bias cannot produce per-cell 1-bit codes; usable only if the code is the same for all switched cells (our 'switched' group, which is switched together in ON/OFF) |
| 3 | In-plane high-impedance narrow traces ("choke-free"), routed along one axis, no vias | none confirmed (#16 snippet only, source blocked) | check what field orientation the trace follows relative to the TM surface wave |
| 4 | Row or column buses with DC-block capacitors and RF chokes | #9: measured gain below simulation, attributed in part to the bias lines (no number) | #9, #2 (DC-block capacitors prevent shorts between meta-atoms) |
| 5 | Common via electrode (existing vias of an SIW reused as the common electrode) | none | patent US 11909112 as reported by the search snippet; patents are not measurements |
Because both our halves are on the top layer, the shortest route is option 1 with the shared ground as the return. Option 2 is the cheapest if all switched cells may be driven together.
