2. Beams & board

Ports

Multi-port: each feed radiates its OWN rectangular piece of the board (Mishra et al.'s multi-port layout) — pick a layout below, then give each port its own beam(s). Single: the ordinary one-feed design below, unchanged.

Fixed port count: "2×2 small + 1 large" is Mishra's 5-port board by definition. "1×N strip" and "N×M grid" take their port count from the fields below; "Custom rectangles" has one port per table row.

Ports are numbered in a serpentine from the top-right: the top row runs right to left, the next row left to right, and so on. For 2×2 that is P1 (+,+), P2 (−,+), P3 (−,−), P4 (+,−). One feed sits at each sub-aperture centre.

Board

Design method

Each cell's phase is quantized to a small number of reflective states (Mishra et al.'s binary-coded metasurface), instead of a continuous phase/impedance — simpler to fabricate (as few as 2 states), at the cost of some beam efficiency (reported below after synthesis).

The app finds the cell states itself from the Board section's substrate, cell size and frequency: the empty cell plus square patches (1-bit: one square about 180° from the empty cell; 2-/3-bit: 4/8 states, 90°/45° apart). Squares are solved with this project's own method of moments, mesh-corrected and snapped to a 0.05 mm grid. Model-only; verify the pair with one HFSS unit cell and override with "Enter two measured values" if it differs.

Multiplexing

Full aperture: every beam's hologram is superposed everywhere (the default). Sectors: each beam owns its own wedge of the board (nearest to its own φ) — typically higher gain per beam, fewer simultaneous beams per cell.
