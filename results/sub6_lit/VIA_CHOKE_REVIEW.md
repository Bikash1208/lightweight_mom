No subscription access; paywalled papers (Wiley, IEEE, IET) were blocked to automated fetching and are known only from search snippets. Evidence tags: **TEXT** = I read the text (HTML or PDF through `pdftotext`); **SNIPPET** = search abstract only. Every number is tagged SIMULATED or MEASURED as the paper states; "NOT_GIVEN" means the text I read does not give it (nothing is filled from memory).

## Summary (what the literature says)
1. **Choke types in use:** (a) radial / butterfly stub pair on the bias line, spaced lambda_g/4 from the via end or from the cell (most common; 5.5 GHz and 5.8 GHz examples, 2604.25430, 2106.11805); (b) a single radial stub right after the diode plus series chip inductors and DC-block capacitors (2604.04625); (c) zigzag inductor + fan-shaped capacitor on a bias layer next to the ground (2412.04822); (d) meander line that doubles as the bias connection between cells (mmWall, 24 GHz; narrow 0.07 mm line not manufacturable, four series inductors needed, lambda/4 stub as large as the cell); (e) avoid the choke by routing the bias lines through current minima of the resonator (KAUST via-less RIS, 23.5-29.5 GHz, VO2 switches); (f) avoid vias altogether by a parasitic-patch topology with a stub in each bias line (ASU 5.8 GHz RIS, 2109.07763).
2. **Via placement rule:** put the via where the RF current / field of the working mode is minimal: patch centre = zero-field point (2106.11805, 5.8 GHz), centroid of the current region chosen by Floquet analysis and a first-order perturbation law Delta(phase) proportional to |J(r)|^2 Delta Z_via (2604.04625), field nulls of both coding states (inverse-designed 1-bit cell, snippet), a hole in the ground plane under a centre via (2305.18199, L band).
3. **Via effects reported on the cell: very little is quantified.** Reported qualitatively or analytically: via impedance shifts the phase (2604.04625, analytical); a badly placed via gives a narrower bandwidth and a worse reflection coefficient (2604.04625, SIMULATED, no numbers); connecting a bias line directly to the resonator changes its response and adds loss (mmWall, no numbers); long vias together with patch cut-outs excite two adjacent high-order resonances (2.5 f and 3.5 f) which the design uses as the wideband mechanism (2304.11572, SIMULATED, then measured array); diode series R and L shift the resonances and the phase curves (snippet of a reflectarray study). **I found no paper that reports a measured frequency shift or an extra resonance caused by the bias via alone**, and none for surface-wave / leaky-wave / holographic antennas with vias through the ground (only the SIW "via fence" patents). This is the gap relevant to our HFSS via variant.
4. **Choke effect on the cell:** isolation is quoted as |S21| < -12 dB in the band for butterfly stubs against no stub or a single stub (2604.25430, SIMULATED); several papers state "no additional loss" without a number (2106.11805). Choke reactance is said to limit the phase range of the cell (snippet).

## Relevance to our two-half island cell (my inference, not from the papers)
* The most transferable rules are (a) via at the minimum of the working-mode current / field, (b) choke = radial or butterfly stub pair at lambda_g/4 from the via end on the bottom layer, (c) bottom-layer bias line narrow (0.3 mm in #1), (d) keep stub reactance and the diode-side stub short, as they limit the phase range.
* Our HFSS island package (island via at the island centre, disc via 2.35 mm from the sheet) puts the island via where the slot-mode field is concentrated; the lit suggests checking the via-induced shift against the no-via run, which the package already asks for (run pairs I01/I04, I02/I05). No published number exists to compare with: our HFSS result would be new.
* For a surface-wave / holographic board, no source quantifies the extra scattering of a via + ground anti-pad array on the bound mode (only the SIW via-fence patents).
